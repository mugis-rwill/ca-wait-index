The Benchmark Line

One horizontal line at six months. Each province's bar runs from its median patient to its unluckiest tenth. Where that bar crosses the line is the finding.

Each bar runs from zero to that province's 90th percentile, banded by two tests at once: is this wait past the median, and is it past the benchmark? Solid fills are where the two agree — tint below both, orange above both. Hatching marks the band where they disagree, and its colour says which way: orange hatch is past the median but still inside six months, blue hatch is past six months but still inside the median. The label above each bar is the 90th percentile as a multiple of the benchmark. A bar with no solid orange would mean 90% of patients treated within six months; no province manages it in any year.

Where the line came from

diagnostic

CIHI states it as “6 months” for hip and knee replacement, on its Wait Times for Priority Procedures in Canada, 2025 page — 182 days. The data workbook itself never carries the figure; it publishes only “% meeting benchmark”. So the line is drawn on the stated benchmark and then checked against the data two ways, because a threshold we could not verify is a threshold we could not defend.

Check one — recover it. P50, P90 and the benchmark column are three points on one cumulative distribution: the percentiles answer given a share, what wait?, the benchmark column answers the inverse, given a wait, what share? Interpolating between them recovers the threshold each province's own published numbers imply. It lands at 186.7 days.

Check two — run it backwards. Predicting each province's published “% meeting benchmark” from its own P50 and P90 reproduces CIHI's figure within 5 points in 100 of 120 province-years at 182 days (median error 1.6 points). At 112 days it succeeds in 0 of 120 (median error 22.2 points).

112 days is the cataract benchmark, not this one. The same CIHI page reports “cataract surgeries (69%) within the recommended 112 days”. Each priority procedure carries its own benchmark — 112 days for cataract surgery, 28 days for radiation therapy, 48 hours for hip fracture repair, 6 months for hip and knee replacement. The 182 days used here applies to hip and knee replacement only, and the backwards test above rules out 112 for these two procedures on the published numbers alone.
Implied threshold by province — check the assumption
The recovered figure is a diagnostic, not a published one. 182 days is CIHI's stated benchmark and is what the line is drawn on. The 186.7-day recovery is corroboration — it depends on an interpolation assumption about the distribution's shape between the two known percentiles, so it belongs in the methodology, never in a headline. It is broken out per province below so the assumption can be checked rather than trusted, and a province sitting far from the rest is worth a second look at its submission.

Open question — which ratio?

needs your call
“A ratio that shows how many surgeries are supposed to be done by the benchmark time” reads two ways, and I've built both rather than pick one silently.
  • Tail ratio — P90 ÷ benchmark. How many times the benchmark the unluckiest tenth actually waits. Saskatchewan hip 2025 = 2.47×. This is a time ratio and it's what the chart currently encodes.
  • Attainment — CIHI's published % meeting benchmark, and its complement turned into people. This is a count ratio: how many surgeries were actually done inside the threshold versus how many should have been. It's in the tooltips and the summary strip.
The second needs one thing I can't get from the workbook: the target share (commonly 90% for hip and knee). The threshold in days was recoverable from the data; a policy target is not. Tell me if you want that target on the chart and I'll need a CIHI source for it — same rule as the 182 days.

Benchmark drawn at 182 days — CIHI's stated 6-month benchmark for hip and knee replacement, corroborated against the published percentiles as described above. No confidence intervals — CIHI publishes aggregate percentiles submitted by provinces, not case-level data.
